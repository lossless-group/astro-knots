## Why Care?

v0.0.9 cut on **May 27**, the day of the Monthly All-Hands. This release is everything between that session and the next one — **June 24's "Dealflow into Second Brains"** — plus a bet on what comes after the sessions themselves.

The immediate work was getting June 24 ready: a session page, a section-composed slide deck, and — the part that matters most — **turning last month's small poll into something legible.** Only a handful of people answered the May 27 survey, but they answered well (a perfect +100 NPS, and concrete asks like "wire Claude into Affinity/Notion/Drive"). Rather than fake analytics over seven responses, we rendered the *reflection and the asks* as data, and let them frame the next session.

The forward-looking work was bigger: the Dojo keeps generating one-off sessions, but knowledge from them evaporates. So we **started a documentation engine** — how-to content reachable from two doors (the problem you're solving, and the tool you've heard about) — to turn ephemeral session demos into durable, navigable guides. This release is where that engine takes its first breath.

## Key Features

### 1. The June 24 session, end-to-end

The "Dealflow into Second Brains" all-hands got a full session post (`/sessions/2026-06-24_dealflow-into-second-brains`) with a Zoom RSVP, and a section-composed deck at `/slides/2026-06-24_monthly-all-hands` (cover, theme, pre-session signals, network, agenda, three demo slides, town hall, close). Presenters: Marcus Brotman (intake → Notion), Toby Rush (his personal-automation second-brain stack), and Michael Staton (a markdown-notebook primer). The `/slides` "Today" highlight moves forward to it automatically.

### 2. May 27 survey rendered as data — with a trajectory

The post-session reflection survey (insightful **9.6/10**, NPS **+100**) and the open poll's Setupathon/curiosity asks now live in one session-keyed JSON that the session page composes (mirroring the April 29 shape). The deck adds an **April → May satisfaction trajectory** (insightful 9.1→9.6, NPS +75→+100) as deck-local markup — no shared component changes.

### 3. A two-perspective how-to engine (the new thing)

Built on **`@lossless-group/lfm`**, deliberately **not Astro Starlight**. One body of how-to content, two navigation doors:

- **`/use-cases/<slug>`** — start from the problem (the first hub: *Automate Dealflow*, straight from the survey's #1 ask).
- **`/guides/<slug>`** — follow the steps (the first guide: *Put Hermes Agent on Your Dealflow*), each wired to its `use_cases:` and `tools:`.
- **`/recipes`** — an umbrella index in the header listing both perspectives as cards.
- **`/tools/<handle>`** — every registry tool now has an internal page (info + body + the guides/use-cases/people that reference it), and **wikilinks prefer it** over the tool's external homepage.

LFM features render live: callouts, `[[wikilinks]]` (via a site resolver), citations, video/GIF embeds — all through the copied `AstroMarkdown` component. The spec (`context-v/specs/Two-Perspective-How-To-Docs-Engine-on-LFM.md`) records the model and, deliberately, leaves the final tree shape to settle from real content.

## The Story

The arc of this release is **ephemeral → durable.** v0.0.9 was about the *machinery* of a live session (auth, polls, stacks). This release used that machinery for one more session (June 24) — but the bigger realization was that the sessions keep producing knowledge that nobody can find afterward. A founder demos an OpenClaw-into-Obsidian pipeline; three weeks later, someone asks "how do I even start a second brain?" and there's no answer to point them at.

That's the gap the how-to engine fills. And the design fell out of the audience itself: VCs say *both* "I need to automate dealflow" *and* "I've heard I need Hermes Agent." Two doors, one room. We resisted the urge to fully architect it on paper — wrote a provisional spec, then **let the structure emerge from authoring one real slice.** It did: `use-cases/` + `guides/`, tool angle on the existing registry, "recipe" as the cross-nav idea. The wikilink resolver flip (internal-first) turned the content into a navigable loop instead of a set of exits to other people's sites.

Plain three-part semver, since the changelog is public-facing and a single readable number reads clearer than a four-part scheme.
- **Bump `package.json`** from `0.0.9` → `0.1.0` to match the tag when cutting the release.
- **The how-to engine is content + raw render only.** No left-nav / TOC / pager yet (specced, not built). Guides are `publish: false` drafts; the Hermes guide's `[!todo]` blocks need a real run to fill in before publishing.
- **`@astrojs/db` deprecation** is the one looming dependency risk — a Drizzle/libSQL-direct migration of the poll/session/stack layer is the documented next step before the Astro major that removes it.

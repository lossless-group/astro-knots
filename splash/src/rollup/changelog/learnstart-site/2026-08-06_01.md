## Why Care?

Before today, sharing a LearnStart link anywhere — iMessage, WhatsApp, Slack,
LinkedIn — produced a grey rectangle. For a firm whose whole pitch is *we are
the recognized Pre-Seed arm of Learn Capital*, the unfurl was doing the opposite
of that work.

There are now four cards, and they carry the positioning in three beats —
wordmark, promise, proof:

> **LearnStart** · Pre-Seed & Seed
> **From mindset to grindset.**
> Transforming the way the world learns.

## The Interesting Part — the card is a page, not a picture

The obvious way to build share imagery is to draw it: a canvas library, a font
file, a pile of x/y coordinates. We started there and threw it away.

Instead the card lives at `src/pages/og/[format].astro`, and it is composed of
the site's own parts — the real `FlameMark` component (same geometry as the
favicon, seed 15), the real `--gradient-accent` token, the real Inter and
JetBrains Mono webfonts. `scripts/shoot-og-cards.ts` only *photographs* those
pages, with headless Chrome at each card's exact pixel size so nothing is ever
scaled between markup and delivered JPEG.

The payoff is that the share card can't drift from the brand. Re-point
`--grad-from` in `theme.css` and the flame on the card moves with it, because
it's the same custom property the site reads. There is no second copy of the
brand to forget to update.

## Two Layers, and the Split Is the Discipline

Ideogram generates the *field* — wordless flame ribbons rising from a low
horizon, on the navy-and-forest palette read straight out of `DESIGN.md`. It
never draws a letter; the negative prompt spends tokens forbidding it. Every
locked channel (palette, seed, negative prompt, rendering speed) lives in
`DESIGN.md`'s `imagery:` block, so only the prompt and the aspect ratio vary per
run and a generation six months from now lands in the same visual family.

Everything legible — flame, wordmark, eyebrow, headline, sub — is markup
composited on top.

The chosen field went through two rounds of curation before it landed: a
radiant burst rising off the right edge, rays fanning left across the frame,
which is what the veil is tuned around.

## Four Formats, Because Chat Clients Crop

WhatsApp and iMessage are the primary targets, so `BannerTall` (1080×1440) is a
first-class deliverable, not an afterthought. Wide cards put copy **beside** the
subject behind a left-to-right veil; tall cards put it **above**, because chat
clients aggressively top-crop tall previews.

Two source fields exist rather than one: cropping the 16:9 field into a 3:4 frame
threw the flames away entirely. Generate at the ratio you'll actually use.

The tall field is deliberately a *different composition* from the banner's — a
dark sky over flame ribbons along the bottom, rather than the banner's burst off
the right edge. That's not an oversight. A tall run aimed at matching the burst
produced four unusable candidates, every one with a bright sky where the cream
headline has to sit. Same palette, same flame motif, different composition, and
legible — which beats matching and unreadable.

## Two Prompt Lessons, Learned Expensively

Both are now encoded in `DESIGN.md` so the next run doesn't rediscover them:

- **A "left half … right half …" prompt renders a literal diptych** — a hard
  vertical seam splitting the canvas into two unrelated panels, in all four
  candidates. The fix is to say *"one single continuous unbroken field"* and keep
  `split screen, diptych, vertical divider` in the negative prompt.
- **Empty space is never left as residue.** It has to be declared first, as its
  own renderable thing with concrete content ("the top 1/2 is empty negative
  space"), or the subject grows to fill the canvas and swallows the zone the
  overlay needs.

## Also Included

`BaseLayout` now emits the full unfurl surface — canonical URL, `og:url`,
`og:site_name`, `twitter:card`, and the image dimensions. `og:image` is absolute
(built from `site` in `astro.config.mjs`), because scrapers fetch it out of
context and reject relative paths. The cards are JPEG rather than WebP: iMessage
and WhatsApp are the least forgiving previewers, and `og:image:type` has to match
the actual bytes or the unfurl silently fails.
